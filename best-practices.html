Recommended opportunity Beginner AI Workflow & Automation
Automate meeting notes → action items, end to end

Record, transcribe, summarize and push action items into the tracker automatically — the single biggest unclaimed win in the whole survey.

Why it matters

Meetings & calls eat 6.8 hrs of a 40-hour week (17%) — and today none of it touches AI. This is the largest untouched block on the schedule.

Problem it solves

Manually re-listening to catch action items, or losing them entirely between meetings and Jira.

Recommended workflow
  1. Record with your calling tool's built-in transcript (Teams Premium, etc.)
  2. Drop the transcript into Claude with a fixed prompt: summarize, extract decisions, extract action items with owners
  3. Push the action items straight into Jira / your tracker
  4. Skim once for anything the transcript-bot mis-heard — don't re-read the whole thing
UX example

A design review with PM + 2 devs generates 6 action items across 3 owners. Instead of writing them up after, the summary + tagged owners lands in Jira within 5 minutes of the call ending.

Before AI → With AI
Before15–20 min per meeting writing up notes, often the same evening or next day, often incomplete.
With AISummary and action items ready before you've closed the call window. One respondent runs meeting questions back through Claude to double-check follow-ups.
Expected benefit

⚡ Speed · ~1.4 hrs/week freed, by the report's own Lever-1 estimate

Feed the design system before you ask for UI

Before asking AI to design or redesign a screen, hand it the design-system rules, existing patterns and constraints — not just the user goal.

Why it matters

It's the single most-repeated cause of wasted time in the whole survey — more than eight people independently named "AI ignored our design system / Lyra standards" as their biggest time-sink, not hallucination or bugs.

Problem it solves

Generated screens that look plausible but don't match spacing, tokens, component states or accessibility rules — requiring more manual rework than designing from scratch would have taken.

Recommended workflow
  1. Before any UI-generation prompt, paste in: the user goal, the business constraint, the relevant design-system components/tokens, and what "out of bounds" looks like
  2. Ask for the component/pattern by name where one already exists — don't let AI invent a new one
  3. Treat the first output as a layout sketch, not a final surface — expect one context-correction pass
UX example

Instead of "design a settings screen with role-based views," specify: "using the Lyra Tabs and Table components, primary/BCP tenant distinction must stay visually explicit per our region-mapping rules, admin/regular-user/guest each map to this permission matrix: [table]."

Before AI → With AI
Without contextClean-looking flow that ignores tenant/region rules — one respondent spent more time revising the diagram than modeling the permissions matrix would have taken up front.
With contextFirst draft respects the constraint; correction pass is cosmetic, not structural.

Read the raw source before you trust the synthesis

AI finds patterns; you find meaning and catch what it missed. Never let a synthesis go out the door unread against its source.

Why it matters

One respondent's synthesis missed a subtle but critical pain point that only showed up indirectly across sessions — they'd accepted the AI output without checking, and a stakeholder caught it in the room. Re-doing it cost more time than reading the transcripts up front would have.

Problem it solves

Confident-sounding but shallow synthesis — AI surfaces the loudest, most frequent signal, not necessarily the most important one.

Recommended workflow
  1. Let AI produce the first-pass themes, quotes and structure
  2. Read the raw transcripts / notes yourself — in full, not skimmed
  3. Specifically hunt for anything contradictory, emotionally loaded, or mentioned by only one person — these are what AI under-weights
  4. Only then finalize
Before AI → With AI

With validationSame speed on production, plus a deliberate re-read pass — "AI drafts, I always read the raw transcripts," as one advanced respondent put it.
Expected benefit

✓ Quality · avoids costly late-stage corrections

Recommended opportunity Advanced Analysis & Synthesis
Cross-check one AI tool's output with another

Run the same analysis through two tools — or through AI and a dedicated tool like Dovetail — and let the disagreement point you at what needs a human decision.

Why it matters

A single AI output can be confidently wrong. Two independent passes rarely fail in the same place — the gap between them is where your judgment is actually needed.

Problem it solves

Over-trusting one model's read on ambiguous or domain-specific material.

Recommended workflow
  1. Run research synthesis in Dovetail (or a second tool)
  2. Pass its output to Claude for cross-validation and gap analysis
  3. Wherever the two disagree, that's the item a human reviews — not the items they agree on
UX example

One respondent moved from Dovetail-only synthesis to a Dovetail + Claude pairing specifically because Dovetail alone "did a lousy job" on its own — the second pass caught what the first missed.

Expected benefit

✓ Quality · catches errors neither tool alone would surface

Default to an interactive HTML prototype, not a static Figma flow

For anything time-boxed or behavior-heavy, build a clickable HTML prototype with AI instead of static screens — it's already what most PMs expect to receive.

Why it matters

Interactive HTML prototyping is the most-cited "couldn't do this before AI" capability in the entire survey — 15+ of 37 respondents named it unprompted, from demo prep to full permission-state flows.

Problem it solves

Static mockups force stakeholders and developers to infer behavior from a slide deck; an interactive prototype lets them experience it.

Recommended workflow
  1. Define the flow and states in natural language, including edge cases and permission/role variants
  2. Describe components, states and behaviors explicitly (see Practice 2 — feed the design system first)
  3. Generate the working prototype, then iterate on specific states in follow-up prompts
  4. Share the link directly with PM/dev — no Figma round-trip needed at this stage

Hand off a structured .md spec, not just an annotated Figma file

Generate a Markdown spec covering variants, states, interactions and edge cases that developers can build from without asking follow-up questions.

Why it matters

Development velocity has outpaced design's ability to keep every ticket detailed by hand — a written, structured spec lets developers move independently on standard work while designers focus on the complex, judgment-heavy features.

Problem it solves

Ambiguity at handoff, and the "moderate to heavy back-and-forth" that 15 of 37 respondents still report before dev accepts AI-touched work.

Recommended workflow
  1. Describe the component/feature to AI in design terms: purpose, variants, states, interactions, content rules, accessibility, edge cases
  2. Generate the .md spec structured for developers, not designers
  3. Review for accuracy — especially the edge cases, where AI most often gets it wrong
  4. Share the .md directly; developers work from it independently
Expected benefit

⚡ Speed · 2–4× · fewer dev round-trips

Budget review time on purpose — never ship the first draft

Plan for a review pass on every AI output before it goes anywhere, instead of discovering the rework mid-task.

Why it matters

65% of the team already reviews with minor edits and 35% still significantly reworks — either way, 100% of the team edits before shipping. Nobody uses AI output as-is. Problem it solves

Validation time that isn't planned for gets skipped under deadline pressure — which is exactly when a bad AI output slips through.

Recommended workflow

  2. Fact-check anything that will be shown to a stakeholder or shipped to dev — personas, competitor claims, user flows — before it leaves your hands
  3. If a task keeps needing "just one more prompt," that's the signal to switch to manual (see Practice 5's anti-pattern)

Force genuinely divergent directions, not five variations of one idea

Ask explicitly for options that differ in approach, not just in styling — and don't let AI default to the obvious answer first.

Why it matters

Design ideation is one of two activities the team already uses AI on beyond its own self-rated automatable ceiling (−18) — the risk isn't too little AI ideation, it's convergent, samey AI ideation without a forcing function for real divergence.

Problem it solves

Settling on the first workable idea because the options in front of you all look like small variants of it.

Recommended workflow
  1. Feed the design brief, user goal and constraint
  2. Explicitly request 5–8 distinct directions, each with its core concept, what makes it different, and its main tradeoff
  3. Use the output as a divergence starting point — not a final answer — then generate component variants for the direction chosen
Expected benefit

↑ Options · 2–3× more directions considered

Synthesize every research signal in one sitting, not one source at a time

Pull Confluence, Jira, Dovetail, competitive analysis and customer feedback into one unified pass instead of reviewing each source separately across a week.

Why it matters

Decisions used to get made on partial information because full synthesis across every source "rarely happened" in a week — now it's realistic to do it in a day, so nothing gets deprioritized for lack of time.

Problem it solves

Design decisions driven by "the loudest single signal" instead of the full picture, because synthesizing every source by hand simply took too long.

Recommended workflow
  1. Export the relevant slice from each source: Confluence, Jira, Dovetail, competitive notes, customer feedback
  2. Feed them into one AI session and ask for a unified view — themes across all sources, not source-by-source summaries
  3. During usability testing specifically, start structuring findings between sessions rather than waiting for the last one to end

Pilot AI-assisted usability-test analysis

Of six UX phases, Validate is the one where the team uses AI least — and the one measured gap where "automatable" still outpaces "automated."

Across Discovery, Synthesis, Define, Design, Validate and Handoff, Validate is the phase where the fewest people apply AI significantly or fully — most report "a little" or "moderate" use, some none at all. Usability-session findings that take longer to tag and rank by severity than the sessions themselves took to run.

  3. A human still runs the session and makes the call on ambiguous or emotionally loaded moments — AI can't read faces or tone, as one respondent noted
  4. Compare time spent against your current manual process and report back

Write microcopy for the following UI states: Component: [name] Product tone: [e.g., professional, direct, reassuring] User context: [what the user just did / is trying to do] Write copy for: - Default label / placeholder - Success state - Error state (what went wrong + how to fix it) - Empty state (no data yet) - Loading state Keep it under [X] characters per state. Avoid generic phrases like "Something went wrong." Be specific to what the user is doing.

✅ Do's & Don'ts Based on survey data + Champion experience
✅ Do
⚡Use AI for the production work — spec writing, synthesis, annotation, microcopy. These are the highest-ROI tasks.
📖Always read raw transcripts yourself. AI finds patterns — you find meaning. Never skip the human validation step.
✏️Treat AI output as a first draft. 65% of outputs need minor edits, 35% need significant rework. Budget time for review.
🔄Use multiple tools to validate each other. One AI's output checked by another catches errors neither would catch alone.
📤Share what works. If a prompt saved you 2 hours, add it to the library. Zero people currently share nothing — let's keep it that way.
🎯Give AI specific context. The more specific the prompt (product, user type, constraint), the less rework you'll need.
🧩Feed the design system before any UI prompt. Component names, tokens and what's out of bounds — this is the single most-named cause of wasted time in the survey. See Practice 2.
❌ Don't
🚫Don't use AI output as-is. 0% of the team does — and that's right. Always review before sharing externally.
🚫Don't let Dev or PM use AI to build UI before UX is involved. AI-generated UI without UX input creates irreversible design debt.
🚫Don't paste confidential customer data into public AI tools without checking the company AI data policy first.
🚫Don't use AI to skip the thinking. Use it to compress production time. Judgment, prioritization, and decision-making stay human.
🚫Don't work in isolation. The biggest waste right now is everyone discovering the same things independently. Use the shared library.
🚫Don't ignore quality just because it was fast. Speed and quality aren't opposites — but AI can make it easy to miss problems at scale.
"AI eliminated production time. Thinking time stayed the same. What didn't compress: running sessions, prioritizing recommendations, reading transcripts yourself, making judgment calls." — Yaara Bar Feldman

  • 2. Validating AI synthesis against the raw source — what separates senior from junior AI use (Practice 3)
  • 3. Building and sharing your own prompt library — the single most-requested fix in the open comments (Practice 7)
